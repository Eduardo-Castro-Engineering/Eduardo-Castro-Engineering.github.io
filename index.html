NASA L’SPACE Mission Concept Academy · Team 10

Venus Aerobot — Thermal Control Subsystem

Lead thermal engineer on a 12-person team designing a balloon powered probe to drift over Venus' Maat Mons and measure volcanic gases. The subsystem went through a full preliminary design review.

Payload Operating Temperature300 K
Surface temperature460 – 470 °C
Float altitude50 km
Ambient pressure≈ 95 atm
Cooling loop draw≤ 83 W
Mass budget50 kg
Show ▼
  • The problem: hold the instrument bay near room temperature while floating 50 km above a surface at 460 – 470 °C, in an atmosphere that is 96.5% carbon dioxide at roughly 95 times Earth’s pressure.
  • The design: a silvered reflective outer skin, a 10 cm insulation blanket, an active refrigeration loop drawing at most 83 W, and a small phase change heater for the cold case. All while adhering to a 50 kg, 1 m³, 36-hour mission budget.
  • Produced the requirements, the trade studies, interface control documents, N² diagrams, and about 20 CAD parts (balloon, canister, helium tank, pump, thermostat, fan, etc.).
  • Carried the subsystem through a full Preliminary Design Review. Certificate of completion signed by the Principal Investigator of NASA’s Lucy mission, with badges in heat transfer, systems engineering, requirements and risk management.

What you are looking at. Water flows left to right over a rough plate; the wall is held at 150 °C in the “heater on” case and left unheated in the “heater off” case. Colour is the local time-averaged velocity — U along the flow, V across it. The third view is the two subtracted, which isolates what the heat alone did to the flow.

Provenance.
